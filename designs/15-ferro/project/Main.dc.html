Does letting volatility move price options better? For fitting today's prices, yes. For reading its settings, no.

Same surface, different magnets.

Double Heston prices options by letting volatility move, and it fits real NSE prices well. Here is Bharti Airtel's option surface at the close on 21 Aug 2026, fitted twice. Both fits miss the market by ₹0.67 an option on average. One says a volatility shock takes 1.1 yr to fade by half; the other says 9 days to 37 days.

Underneath, the ten settings look nothing alike.

Ferrofluid takes its shape from magnets you can't see, and different magnets can raise the same surface. Option prices are the surface; the model's ten settings are the magnets.

Spike height is the size of each setting on its own scale. * pressed against the fit's upper search limit for that setting.

Where the market trades, they agree. A year out, they're 11.7 points apart.

That day Bharti Airtel had options expiring in 4 and 39 days, and both fits price them alike. Ask them about a one-year option, which isn't traded, and fit A says 33.6% volatility while fit B says 21.9%. The prices give no reason to prefer either.

Ask it which of its ten settings produced the bend, though, and the prices can't tell you.

This surface had 6 equally good fits out of 16 starts, and their spread, 4.64, is the median of all 2,400 surfaces. It is the typical case, not the worst. On 99% of 2,400 real NSE option surfaces, several different settings priced the options equally well.
