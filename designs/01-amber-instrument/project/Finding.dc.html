fit(prices) ≈ exact
recover(settings) = no

A perfect price fit is not the same as knowing the settings.

Proof one: simulated surfaces, where the right answer is known

We generated 60 option surfaces from known settings and asked a classical optimizer to find them again, from 4 starting points each. It matched the prices almost exactly: a median price error of 9.16 × 10⁻⁸. The settings it found scored 1.80 on recovery, where 1.00 is what you'd get by always guessing the typical value, and lower is better.

9.16 × 10⁻⁸median price error
1.80recovery skill (1.00 = guessing)
1.85on the 49 closest price fits

A neural network trained to read the settings from prices scores 0.80 on the RMSE-based version of the same score, where the optimizer scores 3.00. It does better by leaning toward typical values; the one setting it reads well is today's level (v₀, 0.47).

Proof two: real nse surfaces, where no answer is assumed

On 2,400 real surfaces (40 of NSE's most traded stocks over 60 days) we started the fit from 16 places each time. On 99% of them, more than one set of settings priced the options equally well, within 10% of the best fit's price error. Those equally good fits sat a median 4.64 apart, against 1.18 for two settings picked at random from the training data: 3.9 times as far.

99%of 2,400 surfaces had several equally good fits
3.9×further apart than two random parameter sets
23%less price error than one flat volatility, median

Split by setting, the pattern is physical. Today's level (v₀) is pinned down: equally good fits agree on it more closely than random sets would. The speeds back to normal (κ) are not pinned down at all; the best fit's speed sat at the edge of the allowed range on 45% (slow) and 73% (fast) of surfaces.
