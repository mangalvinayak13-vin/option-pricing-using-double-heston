Volatility is a random walk too.

For fitting today's prices, yes. Real option prices bend: protection against a fall usually costs more than a bet on a rise. Double Heston bends the same way, and on all 2,400 real NSE option surfaces we tested, its best fit beat one fixed volatility.

Every grey line is one possible year for a stock priced at 100 today, simulated by the project’s Monte Carlo engine at the model’s starting settings. The band holds the middle 50% and 90% of 400 paths.

Two random volatilities, not one

Each factor is a volatility that gets knocked around and drifts back to normal. One drifts back slowly, one quickly, which lets the model shape short- and long-dated options differently.

The settings are random too, in a way

On 99% of 2,400 real NSE option surfaces, several different settings priced the options equally well. They sat 3.9 times further apart than two settings picked at random from the model's training data. The market pins down today's volatility; it can't tell how fast volatility returns to normal.
